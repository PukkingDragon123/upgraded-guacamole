Why fish love the open web

A completely made-up report on the swimming habits of pixel fish found between paragraphs.

Every morning, long before the first browser tab is opened, a small school of loafer minnows gathers along the left edge of the screen. They wait patiently for a page to load, then dart in to explore its headings, its images and the quiet white space between them. Nobody knows why they prefer serif fonts, but they do.

Researchers have noticed that shy fish rarely stay in plain sight. When the cursor moves too fast they bolt for the nearest paragraph and slip behind the lines of text, peeking out only when things calm down again. A long article is, to a fish, a coral reef made of words.

Swimming around pictures

Images are a different matter. A photo is solid and bright, and a fish in a hurry will swim around it rather than through it. Deeper fish, the ones far from the glass, drift behind pictures entirely and reappear on the other side, which is why some readers report fish "teleporting" across a gallery.

"I opened a recipe for soup and a pufferfish inflated right over the ingredients list. Ten out of ten." - a satisfied reader

Field notes

  • Fish never read the page. They only see the outlines of boxes.
  • Scrolling creates a current; a hard flick makes everyone dizzy.
  • A fish that swims off the edge may turn up in another tab.
  • Fishing mode turns the mouse into a hook. Press Esc to stop.

Some species are attracted to crumbs. Tear a tiny scrap off a page, tie it to the hook, and wait for the nibbles. Click too early and the fish swims away offended; click right on the bite and the real fight begins: keep the line tight but not too tight, pull against the fish, and finally draw a net around it.

Caught fish are kept in a small bucket inside the browser. When you open FISHTOP, you can send the whole bucket over and the fish will swim into your aquarium. Fish caught on the web are just as real as fish caught on the desktop, if a little more distracted.
